7 Signs You Have Outgrown QuickBooks (and 2 That Mean You Have Not)

Quick answer

You have outgrown QuickBooks when workarounds, not features, take over your week: spreadsheets that hold the real numbers, data typed twice, and a close that takes days. Count the signs below. Four or more suggests it is time to look at an ERP such as Business Central. Fewer than that, or one of the two "stay" signs, means you may not need to move.

The owner of a growing wholesaler says QuickBooks "still works." Then she lists what happens each month: orders re-keyed from email, inventory tracked in a spreadsheet, and a close that waits on one person's laptop. The software works. The business around it has moved past it. Outgrowing a tool rarely looks like failure. It looks like extra steps.

What are the seven signs you have outgrown QuickBooks?

Give yourself one point for each sign that is true today.

  1. The real numbers live in spreadsheets. QuickBooks holds the ledger, but decisions come from Excel files that someone rebuilds each month.
  2. You type the same data twice. Orders, invoices or timesheets are entered in one system and again in another.
  3. Inventory does not match. Stock counts, costing or availability are managed outside the books.
  4. You need more than one entity or currency. Consolidating companies or handling foreign currency takes manual work.
  5. Approvals happen in email. Purchase orders and payments are approved by reply, with no record in the system.
  6. Reporting takes days. You cannot answer a question by department, project or location without building a report by hand.
  7. One person holds the process. If they leave or fall ill, the close stops.

What are the two signs you have not outgrown it?

You have likely not outgrown QuickBooks if your close is calm and your reports answer your questions without side files. Add to that if your growth plan stays close to the size you are today.

If neither of the two signs fits, but you scored low on the seven, stay where you are and revisit in a year.

How do you score the result?

Count the signs. Zero to three: stay and tidy your current setup. Four to five: start a serious evaluation. Six to seven: the workarounds are costing you now, so plan the move.

Weight the score by time. A sign that costs one hour a month matters less than one that costs a person-day each week.

What happens if you wait?

Workarounds multiply with volume. A process that is annoying at ten orders a day can break at fifty. Waiting is fine when the numbers say it is. It is expensive when you are guessing.

What should you do before you move?

Clean your data in QuickBooks first, since errors move with you. See our post on preparing QuickBooks data for Business Central, published Nov 25.

Then write down the top five workarounds you want gone. They become the requirements for the project.

Taylor’s Take

Do not leave QuickBooks because it is small. Leave it when your business needs something it was not built to do and your people are paying the difference in hours. Count the hours first.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should a business switch from QuickBooks to an ERP?

When workarounds take real time each week, and growth will add more. The signs above give you a way to count them.

Is Business Central a replacement for QuickBooks?

For many companies, yes. Business Central covers accounting, inventory, sales, purchasing and more in one system.

How long does a move from QuickBooks to Business Central take?

It depends on scope, data quality and how available your team is. Ask any partner for a plan with phases, not one number.

Do I have to move everything at once?

No. Some companies start with finance and add other areas later. Plan the chart of accounts and dimensions for the whole business from the start.
