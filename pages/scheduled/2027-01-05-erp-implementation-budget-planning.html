ERP Budget Planning: What Finance Should Include for a 2027 Switch

Quick answer

An ERP budget needs more than license and implementation lines. Add staff backfill, data cleanup, parallel running, integrations, training, post-go-live support and a contingency. These are the lines most plans miss, and they are where overruns come from. Ask your partner to price each one, and decide your contingency before the project starts.

A finance team writes an ERP budget in January: licenses, plus the partner's implementation quote. Six months later the project is over budget, and nobody can say which line went wrong, because the real costs were never in the plan. Staff time, parallel running and training are real money. They just do not arrive as invoices.

What belongs in an ERP budget?

Include every cost layer, not only the two on the quote. Use this list as a starting point:

LineWhat it covers
LicensesSubscription for each user type, from the start date you need
ImplementationDesign, configuration, testing, go-live support
Data migrationCleanup, mapping, loading and verification
IntegrationsConnections to other systems, built and maintained
TrainingSessions for each role, materials and refreshers
Staff backfillCover for people spending time on the project
Parallel runRunning old and new systems together for a period
SupportHelp after go-live, and your internal admin time
ContingencyA reserve for the unexpected

Which lines do most plans miss?

Staff backfill, parallel running, integrations and contingency are missed most often. People assume the team will "fit it in". They cannot, and the cost shows up as overtime, delays or errors.

Ask each department head how many hours per week their people will give. Then price the cover.

How much contingency should you set?

Set it before you start, and agree who can release it. There is no universal percentage. The right level depends on how clear your scope is, how clean your data is and how much change you are making. Ask your partner what contingency they would recommend for a project like yours, and why.

How do you present it to the board?

Show the total, the phases and the assumptions behind it. Include what is not in the budget and why. Boards trust a plan that names its risks more than one that claims none.

How do you keep it on track?

Review actual spending against the plan every month. Match each change order to a budget line. If a line is consistently over, ask why before approving more.

Taylor’s Take

The license is the smallest surprise in an ERP budget. The surprises live in people's time and in data. Budget for both honestly and the project is much easier to defend.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a typical ERP contingency?

It varies with scope and risk. Ask your partner for a recommendation based on your project.

Should the budget include internal staff time?

Yes, at least as a tracked cost. Even if payroll is not a new expense, the capacity is.

Can I phase the budget?

Yes. Fund the design and the first release, with later phases approved on results.

Who owns the budget?

The business owner, with finance tracking spending and the partner reporting progress.
