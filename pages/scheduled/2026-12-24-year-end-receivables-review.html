Year-End Receivables Review: What to Chase, Reserve or Write Off

Quick answer

For a year-end receivables review, sort every overdue account into three groups: chase, reserve or write off. Use age and relationship as the guide: recent balances with active customers get chased, doubtful older balances may need a reserve, and uncollectible balances may be written off. Confirm accounting treatment with your accountant before you change the books.

It is Christmas Eve. The year closes in a week, and the aged receivables report has a long tail. Some of it is about to be paid. Some of it will never be. The review below is short on purpose, and it should be done with your accountant, because reserves and write-offs are accounting decisions, not just collection ones.

What is a year-end receivables review?

It is a pass through every overdue balance to decide what to do before the books close. The goal is a receivables number you can stand behind.

How do you triage the accounts?

Start with the aged receivables report from Business Central. Sort by amount and age, then place each account in one of three groups:

GroupTypical profileAction
ChaseRecent, active customer, no disputeCall or send a final reminder with a payment date
ReserveOlder, unresolved, outcome uncertainAsk your accountant about an allowance
Write offCustomer gone or amount deemed uncollectibleGet approval and document the reason

The age buckets are your guide, not a rule. A 90-day balance from a loyal customer with a signed payment plan is different from one from a company that closed.

What should you do before year-end?

Contact the "chase" group with a specific request: pay by a date, or confirm a plan. Record each promise. Settle disputes where you can, even if you concede part, so the balance becomes clean.

Send statements to everyone with open items. They often prompt payment from customers who forgot.

What should you ask your accountant?

Ask four things: how to estimate an allowance for doubtful accounts, which write-offs need approval, how tax treats bad debts in your situation, and what documentation they want.

Do not change balances on your own. The rules depend on your reporting framework and jurisdiction.

What should you do in January?

Review the list again once the year has closed. Customers often pay in early January, when their new budgets open. Keep following up on a fixed schedule, so the problem does not return next December.

Taylor’s Take

The tail of your ageing report tells you how the whole year went. If the same names appear every year, your credit policy needs work, not just your collections. Fix it at the front door.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an allowance for doubtful accounts?

It is an estimate of receivables you may not collect. Your accountant sets the method.

Can I write off a balance and still collect it?

Sometimes. A write-off is an accounting step. Ask your accountant about recoveries.

Should I stop selling to overdue customers?

Often a credit hold is wise until they pay or agree to a plan. See our credit management post.

Does Business Central have an ageing report?

Yes, aged accounts receivable reports are available. Ask your administrator which version your team should use.
