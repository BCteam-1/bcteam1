Is January the Right Time to Switch ERP Systems?

Quick answer

January is a good time to switch ERP only if you are ready, not because it is a new year. A cutover at the start of a fiscal year gives a clean break in reporting but lands at the busiest time for finance. Month-end and quarter-end cutovers carry other tradeoffs. Choose based on readiness, workload and what history you need to bring across.

The new year feels like a natural starting line. For an ERP cutover, it is also the week your team is busy closing the old year. The best date is not the one with the nicest symbolism. It is the one where your people are free to test, your data is ready and the cutover does not collide with a deadline.

When can you cut over to a new ERP?

You can cut over at month-end, quarter-end or year-start. Each choice has a different effect on reporting, workload and risk.

How do the options compare?

Cutover pointStrengthWatch for
Start of fiscal yearClean annual reporting with one system per yearYear-end close and audit overlap with go-live
Quarter-endClean quarterly reporting and a natural breakReports that mix two systems within the year
Month-endFlexible timing; smaller change at onceTwo systems in one quarter and year, with more reconciling

Cutting over mid-month is generally harder, since balances must be split and reconciled.

What should you check before choosing?

Check five things: whether your data is clean and mapped, whether testing is complete, whether key people are free, whether you have a rollback plan and whether auditors or lenders need particular reporting.

If any of these is not ready, a later date is cheaper than a rushed one.

What if the year-end close is happening at the same time?

A cutover at year-start means finance handles the old year's close while learning the new system. That is possible with extra help, and difficult without it. Plan who closes the old books and who tests the new ones.

Some companies keep the old system read-only for a period, to answer audit questions.

How long before the date should you decide?

Decide early enough to finish testing with margin. Our implementation timeline post explains the phases. A date set without a tested plan is a hope, not a schedule.

Taylor’s Take

Do not pick the date and then squeeze the project. Pick the readiness criteria and let the date follow. A go-live that waits two weeks for clean data is better than one that opens on time with the wrong balances.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it better to go live at the start of a fiscal year?

It gives tidy annual reporting, but the workload is high. Weigh it against readiness.

Can we run two systems for a while?

Some companies run in parallel for a period. It costs staff time, so decide the length and scope in advance.

What if we miss the date?

Move it. A tested, later date is better than a rushed one.

Who decides the date?

The business owner, advised by finance and the partner, based on readiness checks.
