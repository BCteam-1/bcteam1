NAV to Business Central: Upgrade Your Database or Start Fresh?

Quick answer

Upgrade your NAV database when your customizations are few, the data is clean and the current setup still fits your business. Start fresh when you have heavy customization, years of messy data, or processes you want to redesign. Your NAV version also matters: older versions usually need intermediate steps, so ask a partner to confirm the path.

A manufacturer has run NAV for fifteen years. It has custom code nobody wrote down, add-ons whose vendors have gone, and an upgrade quote that depends entirely on which of those survive. "Upgrade" and "start fresh" are not two sizes of the same project. They are different bets. Choose with three facts in hand.

What is the difference between upgrading and starting fresh?

An upgrade carries your existing database, configuration and customizations forward to a newer version. Starting fresh sets up a new Business Central environment and moves only chosen data. An upgrade keeps your history and your design. A fresh start lets you change both.

What three facts decide the path?

Look at your version, your customizations and your add-ons.

  1. Version: older NAV versions usually cannot go directly to the current Business Central. They typically need to pass through intermediate versions first. Ask a partner to confirm the exact path for your version.
  2. Customizations: count them and note who understands each. Custom code written for older versions often needs rewriting as extensions for the cloud version.
  3. Add-ons: check each vendor for a current Business Central version. An add-on without one is a blocker or a replacement project.

When does an upgrade make sense?

An upgrade fits when the system works, customizations are modest, add-ons have current versions and you do not plan large process changes. It preserves your history and shortens the learning curve.

When does starting fresh make sense?

Starting fresh fits when heavy customization is holding you back, data has accumulated problems, or the business has changed so much that the old design no longer fits. It costs design effort, but it can remove years of accumulated workarounds.

Many companies choose a middle path: a fresh design, with selected history brought across.

What about cloud versus on-premises?

Moving to Business Central online changes who runs the servers and how updates arrive. On-premises Business Central is available, with its own support and update rules. Check Microsoft's lifecycle documentation for the version you plan to use, since support dates change.

Ask your partner what each choice means for your customizations and licensing.

Taylor’s Take

An upgrade is not automatically the cheap option. If the old design is the problem, upgrading carries the problem forward at full price. Review the foundation before you choose the path.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I upgrade directly from any NAV version?

Not usually. Older versions need intermediate steps. A partner can confirm the path for your version.

What happens to my customizations?

They need review. Many have to be rewritten as extensions or replaced by standard features.

Is Business Central 14 still supported?

Check Microsoft's lifecycle documentation for the current position, since dates and policies can change.

How long does a NAV upgrade take?

It depends on version, customization and data size. Ask for a phased plan with a test upgrade before the real one.
