Is Your Accounts Receivable Process Ready for Automation? A 10-Question Test

Quick answer

Your AR process is ready for automation when you can say, in writing, who gets contacted, when, by whom and what happens if they do not pay. Answer yes to at least eight of the ten questions below and automation will save time. Answer no to five or more and write the policy first, because software only repeats the process you give it.

A finance team buys AR automation to stop chasing invoices by hand. Six weeks later the tool sends the same vague reminder to every customer, including the one who is in a dispute. The software worked. The process behind it was never written down. Before you compare tools, run this test. It takes ten minutes and tells you whether you need software, a policy, or both.

What does "ready for automation" mean in accounts receivable?

It means your follow-up rules are clear enough that a person who has never met your customers could carry them out. Automation is that person, repeated thousands of times.

If a new hire could not follow your process from a written page, a tool will not do better.

What are the ten questions?

Score one point for each yes.

  1. Do you have a written collections policy with set contact points, such as a friendly note, a firmer note and a final notice?
  2. Can you name the person who owns collections, even if it is a part-time role?
  3. Do you know which customers pay late on purpose and which pay late by accident?
  4. Is every invoice dispute logged somewhere other than one person's inbox?
  5. Do you record promises to pay with a date, so someone can check them later?
  6. Are customer contact details on the customer card current, including an accounts payable email and not only a general inbox?
  7. Do you send statements on a regular schedule?
  8. Can you pull a list of overdue invoices by age in under five minutes?
  9. Do you know who can approve a payment plan, a write-off or a credit hold?
  10. Do you review overdue balances on a fixed day each week?

What does your score mean?

A score of 8 to 10 means you are ready. 5 to 7 means fix the gaps first, then automate. 4 or fewer means write the policy before you buy anything.

ScoreWhat to do
8 to 10Compare tools. Your gap is time, not process.
5 to 7Fix the no answers first. Most are one-week jobs: update contacts, name an owner, set a review day.
0 to 4Write the collections policy. Use the native Business Central reminders to test it for a month before you buy anything.

What can Business Central do before you buy anything?

Business Central has native reminders, finance charge memos and statements. Microsoft's documentation also says it has tools to automate creating, issuing and sending reminders. Set up Reminder Terms with levels and run them on a schedule for a month.

That month shows you where the native tools stop for your team. Common gaps are deciding which accounts to chase first, tracking promises to pay and disputes next to the follow-up, and sending original invoices with statements. If you hit those gaps, you have a specific reason to look at a tool, not a vague wish for "automation".

Taylor’s Take

Software repeats a process. It does not invent one. Many AR problems that look like tool problems are policy problems, and a team that cannot answer questions 1 to 5 above is not short of software.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if I need AR automation?

Track the hours your team spends on statements, reminders and follow-up calls for four weeks. If the total is small, native Business Central reminders may be enough. If it takes a person-day or more each week, look at automation.

Can I automate AR without changing my ERP?

Yes. Extensions that run inside Business Central add to your existing environment. They do not require a migration.

What should I write in a collections policy?

At minimum: the contact steps and timing, who sends each one, who can approve exceptions, and when an account goes on credit hold. Have your accountant review any interest or fee wording.

Should I automate reminders to every customer?

Not at first. Start with a group of customers whose pattern you understand, check the results, then widen the rules.
