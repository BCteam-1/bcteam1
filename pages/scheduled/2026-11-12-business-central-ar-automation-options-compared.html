Business Central AR Automation: Five Ways to Automate Collections, Compared

Quick answer

There are five ways to automate accounts receivable in Business Central: native reminders, an extension that runs inside Business Central, an add-on built on Business Central reminder levels, an external AR platform that syncs with it, and a payments-led tool. The right one depends on where your team already works and whether your gap is follow-up, payments or reporting. If your team lives in Business Central and needs follow-up discipline, an in-product extension is the shortest path.

A controller opens three tabs to chase one customer: Business Central for the invoice, Outlook for the thread, a spreadsheet for the promise to pay. Business Central AR automation is meant to collapse those into one place. But “AR automation” means five different things in vendor pitches, and they solve different problems. Here is how to tell them apart, what each asks of your team, and which fits a team that works inside Business Central all day.

What does Business Central AR automation actually cover?

It covers four jobs: sending statements and reminders, deciding who to chase first, tracking disputes and promises to pay, and applying payments. Most tools do two or three of them well.

Name your gap before you compare tools. If you cannot say which of the four jobs costs your team the most hours, measure it for four weeks first.

How do you choose between them?

Choose by where the pain is, not by the longest feature list.

  • Nobody sends reminders on a rule: set up option 1 first. It costs nothing extra and exposes your policy gaps.
  • Reminders exist, but nobody knows who to chase first or what was promised: option 2.
  • You need customers to pay online and want payments applied automatically: option 5, or option 4 if you also need collections workflow.
  • Invoice volume is high, or you run more than one ERP: option 4.

Five questions to ask any vendor:

  1. Does it write back to Business Central, or keep its own copy of your data?
  2. What happens to your records if you cancel?
  3. Who sets the escalation rules, and can you change them without the vendor?
  4. Where is a promise to pay or a dispute recorded, and who sees it?
  5. What exactly is installed in your Business Central environment, and can it be removed?

Where does RAP fit, and who should not buy it?

RAP (Revenue Assurance Platform) is BC Team’s AR automation extension for Dynamics 365 Business Central. It installs from Microsoft AppSource and runs inside Business Central. Per BC Team’s product page, it covers automated statements with original invoices attached, collections sequencing by aging bucket, a collector priority queue, dispute and promise-to-pay tracking, and a collections dashboard.

The RAP page lists the Standard tier at $400 per month. See the RAP page for current plans and terms.

RAP is not the right choice if:

  • You are not on Business Central or Dynamics NAV. RAP is built for them.
  • Your main gap is online customer payments. The listed RAP features do not include a payment portal.
  • You have no collections policy. RAP runs a policy. It does not write one.
  • Your invoice volume is low enough that native reminders cover it.
Taylor’s Take

The best AR tool is the one your collector opens before 9 a.m. A tool that lives in a second system competes with the inbox for attention and loses. For a team that already works in Business Central, Taylor would try an in-product option before an external platform.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Business Central have built-in AR automation?

Partly. Microsoft documents tools to automate creating, issuing and sending reminders, but describes the base collections process as mostly manual.

How much does AR automation for Business Central cost?

Native tools come with your Business Central license. RAP’s page lists the Standard tier at $400 per month. Other vendors price by seat or invoice volume, so ask for a quote.

Can I automate AR without changing ERP?

Yes. All five approaches work with your existing Business Central environment. None requires a migration.

Is AR automation worth it for a small finance team?

It depends on how many hours follow-up takes. Track the hours your team spends on statements, reminders and calls for four weeks. If the total is small, native reminders may be enough.
