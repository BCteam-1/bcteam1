Clean Up Open Orders and Unapplied Entries Before the Year Ends

Quick answer

Before year-end, review five things in Business Central: unapplied customer payments, unapplied vendor payments, stale open sales and purchase orders, old open journal batches and uncleared bank items. Each can distort receivables, payables or inventory. Work through them with your accountant and record what you changed.

Year-end exposes small messes. A customer payment sitting unapplied since March makes one account look overdue and another look overpaid. An old purchase order still open makes next year's commitments look bigger than they are. A short cleanup before the books close saves a long explanation after.

Why do open entries matter at year-end?

Open entries change the balances and ageing you report. They can overstate receivables, hide credits, inflate commitments or leave cash unreconciled. Clearing them means your numbers match what actually happened.

Which five checks should you run?

Run these five and record the result of each:

  1. Unapplied customer payments. Open customer ledger entries and look for payments or credits not applied to invoices. Apply them or investigate.
  2. Unapplied vendor payments. Same check for vendors. Look for credits and old open items.
  3. Stale sales orders. Find open sales orders with old dates. Ship, invoice or cancel with the customer's confirmation.
  4. Stale purchase orders. Find open purchase orders that will never receive. Close or cancel them with the vendor's confirmation.
  5. Old journal batches and bank items. Look for unposted journal lines and uncleared bank items from earlier months.

What should you do with each result?

Fix what is clearly an error, and ask before you touch anything unclear. Do not cancel an order a customer still expects. Do not write off a balance without approval.

Keep a short log: what you found, what you did and who approved it. Auditors like logs.

Who should be involved?

Finance should lead, with sales and purchasing confirming order status. Your accountant should review anything that affects the books, including write-offs.

When should you do it?

Do the review in the last two weeks of the year, and again right after the year closes for anything that came up late. Putting it on the year-end checklist makes it routine.

Taylor’s Take

Cleanup is easier when you do a little each month. Use year-end as the backstop, not the plan. A monthly look at unapplied entries removes most of the work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I delete old open orders?

Usually you cancel or close them rather than delete, depending on their status. Ask your administrator.

What if a customer payment cannot be matched?

Contact the customer. Hold it as unapplied rather than guessing.

Does cleaning up change posted history?

Applying and closing entries changes their status, not the posted amounts. Corrections that change amounts need proper correcting entries.

How long does it take?

For a tidy system, hours. For a messy one, days. That tells you something too.
