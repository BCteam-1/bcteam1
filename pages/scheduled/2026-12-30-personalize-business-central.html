Personalize Business Central: Role Centers, Fields and Saved Views Users Can Set Themselves

Quick answer

Business Central users can personalize their own screens: show or hide fields, reorder columns, save filtered views and arrange parts of their role center. These changes affect only that user. Administrators can set defaults for a role or the whole company. Let users tune their own view, and keep shared setup changes with the administrator.

A new user opens a list page with thirty columns, twenty of which they will never need. They scroll, squint and ask a colleague where to find the field they want. Business Central lets each person clean up their own workspace in a minute. Most never learn how, because nobody told them it was allowed.

Who can personalize Business Central?

Any user can personalize their own pages, if their permissions allow it. Personalization changes the user's own view. It does not change data or setup for other people.

Administrators can also personalize for others, through profiles and customization, which is a separate, shared level.

What can users change themselves?

Users can usually make five kinds of change:

  1. Show or hide fields on a page, so they only see what they use.
  2. Reorder and resize columns in lists.
  3. Save filtered views of lists, so a common search is one click away.
  4. Arrange parts of the role center, such as activity tiles and charts, where the role center allows it.
  5. Pin actions and choose what to see on a page to fit the task.

Look for the personalization option in the page's settings or user menu. The exact location depends on the version.

What should stay with the administrator?

Leave anything that changes processes, security, shared setup or what other people see. That includes company-wide defaults, required fields, permissions and role center layouts for a whole role.

Personalization is a view change. It does not make a field required or change a posting rule.

How do you roll this out to a team?

Teach it in training, in plain steps. Ask each user to hide five fields and save one filtered view in their first week. Share good examples, such as the filter a collector uses for overdue accounts.

Then review shared defaults. If everyone hides the same fields, the default page may need a change.

Taylor’s Take

A system that shows people only what they need gets used more. Personalization is free, fast and easy to teach. Make it part of onboarding, and treat repeated hiding of the same field as feedback on your setup.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does personalization affect other users?

No. It changes only the user's own view, unless an administrator applies it to a profile.

Can I undo my changes?

Yes. There is an option to reset your personalization to the default.

Can users hide required fields?

They can hide a field from view, but the field still follows its setup rules. Required data must still be entered.

Do changes survive an update?

They are saved to the user profile, so they normally persist. Test after major updates.
