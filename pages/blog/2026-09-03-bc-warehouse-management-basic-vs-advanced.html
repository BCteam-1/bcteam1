Warehouse Management in Business Central: Basic vs. Advanced, and How to Know Which You Need

Turning on Directed Put-away and Pick is a one-way door on any location with posted transactions. Here's how to know which level actually fits before you flip that switch.

Someone on the IT team turns on Business Central's most advanced warehouse configuration — Directed Put-away and Pick — because it sounds like the more capable, more professional option. Three weeks later, warehouse staff are fighting mandatory bin codes on every single transaction for a 12-person distribution operation that never needed that level of structure in the first place, and it turns out this setting can't simply be turned back off now that real transactions have posted against it.

This is a genuinely common mistake, and it runs in both directions — companies that need real warehouse structure and never configure it, and companies that turn on more complexity than their operation actually requires. Both mistakes are avoidable if the decision is made deliberately, with a clear understanding of what each level actually does.

Business Central Uses "Basic" and "Advanced" Deliberately

Microsoft's own documentation uses these exact terms — basic and advanced — to describe a genuine range of complexity levels in warehouse configuration, not a simple on/off toggle. The most advanced level specifically is Directed Put-away and Pick, activated location by location through a toggle on the Location Card. Everything below that level is considered basic warehousing, even though basic itself spans a real range — from no bin tracking at all, up to bin tracking without the full directed workflow.

What Basic Warehousing Actually Covers

Basic warehousing can still include meaningful structure: bins, simple picks and put-aways, and — as of a relatively recent platform update — even directed pick and put-away suggestions based on put-away templates and bin ranking, without requiring the full advanced toggle. This matters because a lot of companies assume "advanced" is required to get any kind of system-suggested bin logic at all, when Business Central extended a meaningful piece of that capability down into basic configurations specifically to serve operations that don't need the full advanced structure.

What Directed Put-away and Pick Actually Changes

Turning on Directed Put-away and Pick for a location activates a genuinely different operating model: warehouse documents — Receipts, Put-aways, Shipments, and Picks — become required to move inventory through that location at all, decoupling physical warehouse activity from back-office financial posting entirely. A warehouse receipt confirms goods arrived; a separate put-away document confirms exactly where they were stored; nothing can be invoiced or consumed until that full document chain is complete.

This level of structure is genuinely valuable for mid-to-large operations with distinct roles — a receiving team, a separate shipping team, dedicated pickers — where detailed tracking and accountability at each handoff point matters. It's meaningfully more process overhead for a smaller operation where the same two or three people handle receiving, storage, and shipping interchangeably.

The Detail Most Companies Miss: This Is a One-Way Door

Here is the specific detail worth knowing before enabling this setting: once an Item Ledger Entry has been posted in a location configured for Directed Put-away and Pick, that setting cannot be disabled for that location. There's no simple "turn it back off" path once real transactions exist. A company that enables this prematurely, discovers it's more process overhead than their operation actually needs, is left either living with it or migrating to an entirely new location code — a genuinely disruptive fix for a decision that could have been made carefully upfront.

A Practical Way to Decide

Before enabling advanced warehousing for any location, it's worth answering a few honest questions. Does the operation have genuinely distinct roles — separate people responsible for receiving versus picking versus shipping — where accountability at each step matters? Is transaction volume high enough that system-suggested bin placement based on ranking and templates would meaningfully reduce errors, versus a small team that already knows exactly where everything goes? Does the business need to track goods in a receiving zone as distinct from goods available for sale, with formal put-away required between the two?

If the honest answer to most of these is no, basic warehousing — potentially with the newer directed pick and put-away suggestions feature layered on top — usually serves the operation better than the full advanced toggle, without locking the location into a structure that can't be reversed.

Where to Start

If a location is already running Directed Put-away and Pick and it feels like more process overhead than the operation needs, that's a harder conversation requiring a real migration plan, not a quick setting change. If no location has been configured yet, this is exactly the kind of decision worth making deliberately — based on actual operational roles and volume, not on which option sounds more sophisticated.
