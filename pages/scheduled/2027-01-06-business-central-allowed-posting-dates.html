“Posting Date Is Not Within Your Range of Allowed Posting Dates”: Fix It in Business Central

Quick answer

This error means the posting date falls outside the dates Business Central allows for your user or company. Check User Setup first, since a user's own range overrides the company range. Then check General Ledger Setup, then the posting date ranges in the Sales and Purchases setup pages. Change the range only with your finance lead's approval.

It is the first week of January. Your team is posting the last invoices of December, and Business Central refuses with "Posting Date is not within your range of allowed posting dates." Nothing is broken. The system is doing exactly what someone told it to do months ago. The job is to find where that instruction lives.

What does the error mean?

Business Central only lets you post inside a window of dates. If your posting date falls before the start or after the end of that window, it stops the posting. The window protects closed periods from accidental changes.

It is a control, not a fault. Treat changes to it as a finance decision.

Where do you check, and in what order?

Check these places in order, from the most specific to the most general:

  1. User Setup. Search for User Setup. A user's own Allow Posting From and Allow Posting To dates override the company dates. This is the most common source of the problem for one person.
  2. General Ledger Setup. The company-wide Allow Posting From and To dates live here.
  3. Sales and Purchases setup. Other setup pages can have their own posting date limits for documents. Check those if the first two are fine.
  4. Accounting periods. If you use accounting periods, a closed period or fiscal year can stop posting.

Compare your document's posting date to each range. The one that excludes it is your cause.

How do you fix it safely?

Do not widen the range on a whim. First confirm with your finance lead that posting into that period is allowed. If it is, extend the date for the narrowest scope that works: one user, for a short time.

Then post, and put the range back. Record who approved the change.

Why does it happen in January?

At the start of a year, ranges are often still set for the old year, or have been closed after the previous close. Add "set the new year's posting ranges" to your year-end checklist, and decide who can post into the prior year during the close.

How do you prevent it?

Keep a calendar of period opens and closes. Give late-posting rights to named people only. Review User Setup twice a year, because old exceptions linger.

Taylor’s Take

Posting date controls are one of the cheapest audit protections you have. Keep them tight and keep a record of every exception. (If the person who set the range left two years ago, congratulations: you have found a documentation gap.)

Frequently Asked Questions

Why can only one user not post?

Their User Setup range probably differs from everyone else's. Check theirs first.

Can I post into a closed period?

It depends on your setup and your accountant's rules. Ask before you change the range.

Does the error affect saved data?

No. The posting was blocked before anything was written.

Who should change the posting range?

A finance lead or administrator, with approval from whoever owns the close.
