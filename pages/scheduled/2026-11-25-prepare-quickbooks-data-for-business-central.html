How to Prepare QuickBooks Data for Business Central Before You Pay Anyone

Quick answer

Prepare QuickBooks data for Business Central in six steps: clear old open items, merge duplicate customers and vendors, draft a clean chart of accounts, reconcile your balances, remove unused list entries and export what you need to keep. Doing this in QuickBooks first costs less than paying a consultant to clean it during migration.

A migration quote includes "data cleansing" as a line item. It is the line most worth shrinking. Every duplicate customer, stale invoice and mystery account in QuickBooks will either move into Business Central or take billable hours to resolve. Your team knows which customer is real and which account is dead. A consultant has to ask. Start the cleanup now.

What should you fix first in QuickBooks?

Fix the items that are cheapest to fix and most expensive to carry. That means open invoices and bills, duplicate customers and vendors, and the chart of accounts.

What are the six steps?

Work in this order:

  1. Clear old open items. Review unpaid invoices and bills. Collect, write off or apply payments to anything that is stale. Ask your accountant about write-offs.
  2. Merge duplicates. Find customers and vendors entered twice under slightly different names. Merge or retire the duplicates.
  3. Draft the new chart of accounts. Do not copy the old one. List the accounts you actually use, group them, and note what you want to report on. See our chart of accounts review.
  4. Map old accounts to new ones. For each QuickBooks account, write which new account it becomes. This sheet drives the migration.
  5. Reconcile. Reconcile bank and credit card accounts up to the last closed month. Check that sub-ledger totals match the balance sheet.
  6. Remove or archive what you do not need. Inactive items, old classes and unused payment methods stay behind.

What data do you need to move?

Most projects bring master data (customers, vendors, items), open balances and opening general ledger balances. Many leave detailed history in QuickBooks, or in an archive, for reference. Decide early how many years of history you need and why.

Ask your accountant and your partner what the audit and tax rules require for your business. Do not guess.

What should you hand to your partner?

Give them four things: the account mapping sheet, clean customer and vendor lists, an open items list as of a cutoff date, and a note on anything unusual such as multi-currency balances or unusual tax setups.

A short list of known problems is useful. A partner can plan for a flagged problem. An unflagged one shows up during testing.

How long should the cleanup take?

It depends on how long QuickBooks has been in use and how tidy it is. A reasonable plan assigns one owner, sets a date for each step and reviews progress weekly. Start before you sign, since you will do the work either way.

Taylor’s Take

Migration does not clean data. It moves it. The cleaner your starting point, the more of the project budget goes to design and training instead of repair.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I copy my QuickBooks chart of accounts into Business Central?

Usually not. Use the move to design a cleaner structure, then map the old accounts to it.

How much history should I migrate?

Enough to meet your reporting, audit and tax needs. Detailed history can often stay in QuickBooks or an archive.

Who does the cleanup?

Your team, because they know the business. Your partner can review the result.

When should I start?

As soon as you decide to evaluate a move. The steps help even if you stay.
