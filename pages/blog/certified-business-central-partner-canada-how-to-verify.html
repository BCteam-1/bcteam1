How to Find a Certified Microsoft Business Central Partner in Canada

Most buyers are still evaluating partners against a certification system Microsoft retired in 2022. Here's how to verify what a firm's current standing actually is.

If you searched for a "certified Business Central partner" and started comparing companies by whether they call themselves a Gold Partner, you're evaluating against a credential system that hasn't existed since September 2022.

Microsoft retired the Gold and Silver Partner competencies that year and replaced them with Solutions Partner Designations under what's now called the Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program. The change wasn't cosmetic. The old system measured whether a firm had passed a competency threshold once and kept renewing it. The new system scores partners continuously across three categories — performance, skilling, and customer success — and requalifies them annually. A firm that earned Gold status in 2019 and never touched the new framework has no current standing to speak of, whatever their website still says.

This matters more than it sounds like it should, because a fair number of Canadian ERP consulting firms are still running marketing copy written before the change. Some genuinely haven't updated their site. Others are, less charitably, hoping nobody checks. Either way, the practical question for a buyer isn't "does this company call itself certified" — it's "can I verify what they're actually certified for, right now, independently of anything they've told me."

What Changed, Specifically

Microsoft's current partner recognition system groups capability into six Solutions Partner Designations: Data & AI, Modern Work, Business Applications, Infrastructure, Security, and Digital & App Innovation. Business Central falls under Business Applications — the designation relevant to Dynamics 365 and Power Platform work, not the others.

Earning that designation requires a Partner Capability Score of at least 70, built from three weighted categories: performance (customer growth and consumed revenue in that solution area), skilling (the number of team members holding relevant role-based Microsoft certifications), and customer success (verified delivery of intermediate and advanced specializations, plus usage growth among their client base). It's re-evaluated annually, not granted once and forgotten.

There was a transition window. Partners who held active Gold or Silver competencies could keep renewing those legacy benefits for a fee through January 21, 2025 — after that date, Microsoft stopped selling legacy renewals entirely. So as of today, there is no such thing as a currently valid Microsoft Gold Partner. If a firm's website still leads with that language, it's either outdated marketing or a signal they haven't re-qualified under the framework that's actually replaced it.

How to Actually Verify a Partner's Standing

None of this requires taking a partner's word for it. Microsoft publishes partner designations in a searchable public directory, and checking a specific firm takes a few minutes:

  • Search the firm by name in Microsoft's official partner directory and confirm whether Business Applications appears as an active designation, not just a general "Microsoft Partner" listing.
  • Ask directly how many team members currently hold role-based Business Central or Dynamics 365 certifications — not how many did five years ago, and not a headcount for the whole company.
  • Ask what specializations, if any, sit on top of their core designation. Advanced Specializations validate deeper expertise in a specific area and aren't required to hold the base designation, but a firm that has one is signaling something more specific than general capability.
  • If a firm mentions Gold or Silver status without qualifying it as historical, ask directly whether they've transitioned to the current framework. A firm confident in its current standing will answer that plainly.

None of this replaces checking references or reviewing past project work — a designation tells you a firm meets a bar Microsoft has defined, not that they're the right fit for your specific business. But it's a filter worth applying before you get to the harder, more subjective questions, and it takes less time than a single sales call.

What a Designation Does and Doesn't Tell You

A Business Applications designation is a reasonable floor. It tells you a firm has certified staff, active customers, and a track record Microsoft is willing to vouch for in that specific solution area. It doesn't tell you whether that firm has experience in your industry, whether their typical client looks anything like your business, or whether the person who'll actually run your project is the same senior consultant featured in their marketing.

Those questions matter as much as the designation itself, and they're worth asking directly rather than assuming a credential answers them. A firm can hold every current Microsoft designation and still be a poor fit if their usual engagement is a 500-user manufacturer and you're a 30-person distributor — a fit question this series covers separately, since it's a different evaluation entirely from credential verification.

Where This Leaves a Canadian Buyer

The practical version of all this: treat "certified partner" as a claim to verify, not a credential to trust on sight. Check the current designation directly, ask specific questions about certified headcount and specializations, and treat any unqualified Gold Partner language as a prompt to ask a follow-up question rather than a green light.
