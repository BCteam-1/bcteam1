Accounts Receivable Collections Policy: A 30/60/90 Template for Business Central

Quick answer

A 30/60/90 collections policy sets one action for each age band of an overdue invoice, names who takes it and says when an account goes on hold. In Business Central, each band maps to a reminder level. Write the policy first, then set the levels, grace periods and fees to match, and have your accountant review any interest or fee wording.

Most teams have a collections routine and no collections policy. The routine lives in one person's head: who gets a call, who gets an email, who gets ignored because they are friendly. A written policy fixes that. It also makes the Business Central reminder setup easy, because the levels are just the policy, typed in.

What goes into a collections policy?

Five things: the age bands, the action at each band, the owner of each action, the exceptions process and the credit hold rule. Keep it to one page.

What does a 30/60/90 template look like?

Treat this as a starting point. Adjust the days, tone and fees to your business and your customers' terms.

How do reminder levels match the template?

Each reminder level has a grace period, optional fee, optional interest and its own text. Set the grace period for each level to the number of days after due date when it should apply. Write the level text to match the tone in your policy. Our reminders setup guide has the steps.

Before you add fees or interest, check the wording on your invoices, your customer agreements and local rules with your accountant or lawyer.

What manual work does each level create?

The reminder run drafts documents, but people still review, issue and follow up. Level 1 is mostly routine. Level 2 needs calls. Level 3 needs judgment and approval. Count how many calls and notes this creates each week, and you will see where time goes.

Promises to pay and disputes also need tracking, and the standard reminder tools are not built around them. That gap is where many teams look at automation.

How do you roll out a policy?

Share it with sales first. Customers often hear about payment terms from them, and sales sometimes undermine collections. Agree on the exception process and who may approve a delay.

Then start with a pilot group of customers and review after a month.

Taylor’s Take

The policy is the product. Software and reminders only deliver it. A short policy that everyone follows beats a perfect one that lives in a drawer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should every customer follow the same schedule?

Not always. Many companies use tiers by size, history or terms. Start simple and add tiers when you need them.

Can I charge late fees?

That depends on your agreements and local rules. Ask your accountant or lawyer before you add fees or interest.

When should an account go on credit hold?

Set a rule in the policy, such as a number of days past due or a balance limit, and apply it consistently.

How often should I review the policy?

Once a year, or when results change.
