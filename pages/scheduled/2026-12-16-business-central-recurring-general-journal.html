Recurring General Journals in Business Central: Stop Retyping Monthly Entries

Quick answer

A recurring general journal stores entries you post on a schedule, such as rent, insurance or accruals. You set a recurring method (fixed or variable), a recurring frequency and, if needed, an expiration date. Each period you open the journal, adjust any variable amounts and post. It replaces retyping the same lines every month.

Rent is $4,200 on the first of every month. Someone on your team types that entry twelve times a year, and once a year gets the account wrong. Business Central has a journal built for this exact job. It keeps the lines, remembers the schedule and lets you change only what changed. Here is how to set it up.

What is a recurring general journal?

It is a general journal whose lines are saved as a template and reused each period. Unlike a normal journal, the lines stay after you post. The posting date moves forward by the frequency you set.

Search for Recurring General Journals to find it.

How do you set one up?

Create a batch, add lines and set the recurring fields:

  1. Create a batch for the type of entry, for example MONTHLY.
  2. Add the journal lines: account, description, amount and balancing account.
  3. Set the Recurring Method: Fixed keeps the same amount each time. Variable lets you enter the amount each period.
  4. Set the Recurring Frequency with a date formula, such as 1M for monthly.
  5. If the entry should stop, enter an Expiration Date.
  6. Post, then check the new posting date on the lines.

What are good examples?

Three examples cover most needs:

  • Rent (fixed): same amount, same accounts, monthly.
  • Prepaid insurance (fixed): monthly amortization from a prepaid asset to expense.
  • Utility accrual (variable): estimate each month, edit the amount, post, then reverse the next period according to your policy.

For allocations across departments, Business Central also supports allocation lines in recurring journals. Test those with your accountant first.

What mistakes should you avoid?

Four problems appear often: wrong posting dates after a skipped month, variable lines posted at zero, entries that should have expired but did not, and journals nobody reviews.

Assign an owner to each batch. Put the review in the month-end checklist.

Can recurring journals be fully automated?

Not by themselves. Someone still opens the journal and posts it. Many teams like that, since a person confirms the amounts. If you want less manual work, ask your partner about automation options that fit your controls.

Taylor’s Take

Recurring journals are a small habit with a large effect on close time. A routine entry should take seconds, not minutes. Free your team's attention for the entries that need judgment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between fixed and variable?

Fixed repeats the same amount. Variable keeps the line but expects you to enter the amount each time.

Can I post a recurring journal for a past period?

Yes, if the date is in an allowed posting range. Check the date before posting.

How do I stop a recurring entry?

Set an expiration date or delete the line.

Do recurring journals work with dimensions?

Yes, you can set dimensions on the lines. Check that required dimensions are filled in.
