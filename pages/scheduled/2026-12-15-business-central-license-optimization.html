Are You Paying for Business Central Licenses Nobody Needs?

Quick answer

Audit Business Central licenses user by user. List every licensed user with their role, last sign-in and what they actually do in the system. Users who never sign in can be removed, and users who only view data or enter light information may fit a cheaper license type. Confirm license rules with your Microsoft partner before you change anything.

Licenses are assigned at go-live, when everyone who might need access gets some. Two years later, three of those people have left, five never sign in, and several have a license that is more than they use. The bill still arrives every month. A license audit takes an hour and often pays for itself.

How do you audit Business Central licenses?

Build a list with one row per user. Export the user list from Business Central, then add what the system does not show: their job, whether they are still employed and what they actually do.

Add a column for last sign-in if your tools provide it. Your administrator or partner can help you pull it.

What should you record for each user?

Record the user, role, license type, last sign-in, main tasks and a decision. The decision column is what you came for.

Which license types should you compare?

Business Central commonly offers Essentials, Premium and Team Member license types, plus add-ons. The types differ in what a user can do and which features they can reach. Microsoft publishes the current rules, and they change, so check the licensing guide before you decide.

Compare what each user does against what each type allows. Do not assume that a user who "only looks at things" fits the cheapest type until you check.

How do you make changes safely?

Change licenses in a planned window. Warn the user, change the license, and ask them to test their normal tasks. Keep a list of what you changed so you can reverse it.

Also review permission sets. A user with the right license but too many permissions is a risk, not a saving.

How often should you repeat it?

Twice a year, and whenever staff changes. Add license review to your offboarding checklist, so leavers are removed when they leave.

Taylor’s Take

Licensing is a quiet cost, and an easy one to control. Do not start by trimming. Start by knowing who uses what. A clean user list also helps security, which is a second reason to keep it tidy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I downgrade a license mid-term?

It depends on your agreement and how you buy. Ask your Microsoft partner before you plan savings.

What happens to a removed user's history?

Their past entries stay in the system. Removing or disabling the user does not delete transactions.

Do external accountants need licenses?

There are options for accountant access, with their own rules. Check with your partner and the current Microsoft guidance.

Who should own the license list?

The system administrator, reviewed by finance.
