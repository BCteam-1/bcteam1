Business Central Extensions: How to Audit What Is Installed and What You Still Need

Quick answer

To audit Business Central extensions, open the Extension Management page, list every installed extension with its publisher and version, then sort each into keep, update or remove. For each one, ask who owns it, when it was last used, what it costs and whether it is up to date. Test removals in a sandbox first.

Two years after go-live, nobody can say why eleven extensions are installed. Three came with the original project. Two were tests. One does something nobody remembers. Each extension adds cost, update risk and a possible performance load. An audit takes an afternoon and usually finds at least a few you can retire.

How do you see which extensions are installed?

Search for Extension Management in Business Central. The page lists installed and available extensions with the publisher, version and status.

Export or copy the list into a spreadsheet. That sheet becomes your audit record.

What should you record for each extension?

Record six facts: name, publisher, version, business owner, last known use and annual cost. Most teams cannot fill in the owner and last-use columns at first, and that is the finding.

How do you decide: keep, update or remove?

Keep it if someone owns it, uses it and it is current. Update it if it is in use but behind. Remove it if nobody owns it or uses it.

Be careful with extensions that other extensions depend on. Check for dependencies before you uninstall, and ask the publisher if you are unsure.

How do you remove an extension safely?

Remove it in a sandbox first. Confirm that no process, report or user depends on it, then repeat the steps in production during a quiet period.

Take note of any data the extension stored. Ask the publisher or your partner what happens to that data on uninstall, and export it if you need it.

How often should you repeat the audit?

Once a year at minimum, and again before each major Business Central update. Updates are the moment when an old extension is most likely to cause trouble.

Taylor’s Take

Every extension is a small bet that it will keep working after the next update. Make each one earn its place with an owner and a clear job. An extension nobody can explain is a risk with a subscription fee attached.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do unused extensions slow down Business Central?

They can add load or update risk, depending on what they do. Removing ones you do not need is good housekeeping either way.

What is the difference between an AppSource app and a per-tenant extension?

An AppSource app is published for many customers. A per-tenant extension is custom code written for one environment. Per-tenant extensions need a clear owner for the source code.

Can I uninstall an extension without losing data?

It depends on the extension. Ask the publisher what happens to its data on uninstall, and test in a sandbox first.

Who should own the extension list?

The business owner of Business Central, with the administrator keeping it current. See our post on who owns Business Central after go-live.
