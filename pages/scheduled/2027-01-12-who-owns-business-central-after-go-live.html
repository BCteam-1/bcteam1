Who Owns Business Central After Go-Live?

Quick answer

After go-live, Business Central needs three owners: a business owner who decides what the system should do, an administrator who runs it day to day, and a partner who advises and builds. Write down who does what in a one-page matrix. Without it, decisions stall and the system drifts into the problems the project was meant to solve.

The project team celebrates go-live, packs up and moves on. Two months later, a user asks for a new field. The finance lead thinks IT will handle it. IT thinks it is a finance decision. The partner has not been asked. Nobody owns the answer. A new year is a good time to fix that, before the next request arrives.

Who should own Business Central?

Three roles share ownership. The business owner owns the "why", the administrator owns the "how" and the partner owns the "what is possible". No single person covers all three well.

What does each role do?

RoleOwnsTypical tasks
Business ownerDecisions and prioritiesApprove changes; set the roadmap; own data rules
AdministratorDaily operationUsers and permissions; monitoring; updates; first-line help
PartnerExpertise and buildAdvice; customization; training; complex issues

The business owner is often the controller or CFO. The administrator can be an internal finance systems person or a partner-provided service.

What goes in the responsibility matrix?

List the main responsibilities and mark who decides, who does the work and who must be consulted. Include at least these rows: user access, chart of accounts changes, new dimensions, report requests, extension changes, update testing, backup and recovery checks and training for new hires.

One page is enough. The value is in agreeing it, not in the formatting.

How do you set up a change request process?

Make it simple. Requests go to one place. The business owner approves or declines. The administrator or partner scopes the work. Record what was done and why, so future staff can understand it.

That record is also your protection when someone leaves.

What are the warning signs of no ownership?

Signs include unexplained setup changes, users with access nobody approved, extensions nobody can explain and long waits for simple requests. If you recognize two of these, assign the roles this month.

Taylor’s Take

A system without an owner drifts. Pick the owners, write the matrix and review it every year. This is the cheapest optimization project you will ever run.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can one person be both business owner and administrator?

In a small company, yes. Keep the decisions and the daily work separate in your records.

Do we need a full-time administrator?

Not necessarily. Many small companies use a part-time internal owner with partner support.

What should the partner handle?

Advice, customization and complex issues. Keep routine tasks in-house where possible.

How often should we review the matrix?

Once a year, and whenever key staff change.
