Manufacturing in Business Central: Standard vs. Premium License, What You Actually Get

Premium licensing applies to every full user in the company, not just the people on the shop floor. That detail changes the real cost math more than most companies expect.

A finance team building next year's software budget assumes upgrading to Premium licensing for manufacturing will only cost more for the five people actually running production orders. Then the actual quote comes back, and it turns out every single full user in the company — sales, finance, admin, everyone — has to move to Premium too, because Business Central licenses at the company level, not per-module. The five-person cost estimate was wrong by a wide margin, and it was wrong because of a licensing detail that isn't obvious until someone specifically asks about it.

This is one of the most consequential, least understood details in Business Central licensing for any company evaluating manufacturing capability, and it's worth understanding clearly before it shows up as a budget surprise.

The Core Difference: Two Modules, Not a Feature Tier

Business Central Essentials covers genuinely broad functionality — full general ledger, accounts payable and receivable, fixed assets, sales and purchase order management, inventory, warehouse management, project management, and reporting including Power BI integration. For a large share of companies implementing Business Central, Essentials alone covers everything they actually need.

Premium adds exactly two additional capability areas on top of everything in Essentials: Manufacturing and Service Order Management. Manufacturing includes production orders, bills of materials, routings, work and machine centers, capacity planning, and material requirements planning. Service Management covers service contracts, service orders, and resource allocation for after-sales support and maintenance operations. If your business needs neither of these two specific areas, Premium adds licensing cost without adding any functionality you'd actually use.

The Licensing Detail That Actually Matters

Here is the specific point worth understanding clearly before budgeting: Business Central does not allow mixing Essentials and Premium licenses within the same company. If your business needs Manufacturing or Service Management for even a small subset of users, every full user across the entire company must be licensed at the Premium tier — not just the users working directly in production or service. A ten-person company with two people running production orders and eight people in sales, finance, and administration needs all ten users on Premium, because the platform doesn't support licensing some users at Essentials and others at Premium within a single environment.

This is the single most common source of budget surprise in manufacturing-related Business Central licensing conversations — companies estimate cost based on how many people will actually touch manufacturing functionality, when the real cost driver is total full-user headcount across the entire business.

Assembly vs. Full Production: A Distinction Worth Checking Before Assuming You Need Premium

Not every company that makes or combines physical products needs the full Manufacturing module. Business Central's Assembly Management functionality — available in Essentials, not requiring Premium — handles straightforward assembly or kitting scenarios: combining components into a finished item without complex routing, multi-step production, or capacity-constrained scheduling. A company that assembles kits from purchased components, without genuine multi-step production floor complexity, may be fully served by Assembly Management alone, avoiding the company-wide Premium licensing cost entirely.

The distinction worth checking honestly: if your production process involves multiple sequential operations, machine or work center scheduling, or bills of materials with routing steps that need to be tracked and costed individually, Assembly Management genuinely won't be sufficient, and Premium's full Manufacturing module is the right fit. If your process is closer to combining a handful of purchased parts into a sellable finished unit, it's worth testing whether Assembly Management alone actually covers it before assuming Premium is required.

What This Means for the Budget Conversation

Before committing to Premium licensing for manufacturing, the real cost comparison isn't "Premium price minus Essentials price, times the manufacturing team headcount." It's "Premium price times total company full-user headcount, compared against Essentials price times that same headcount" — a materially larger number for most companies than the narrower estimate they start with. This doesn't mean Premium is the wrong choice for a genuine manufacturing operation; it means the actual cost needs to be modeled correctly from the start, not discovered during a vendor quote.

Where to Start

Before assuming Premium licensing is necessary, it's worth checking honestly whether your production process is genuinely multi-step and routing-dependent, or whether it's closer to assembly and kitting that Essentials' Assembly Management could handle on its own. If Premium is genuinely required, model the cost against your full company headcount, not just the team that will directly touch manufacturing functionality — that's the number that will actually appear on the invoice.
