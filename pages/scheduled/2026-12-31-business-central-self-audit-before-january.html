A 20-Minute Business Central Self-Audit to Run Before January

Quick answer

Answer ten yes or no questions about access, posting, data, reporting and updates. If you answer no to three or more, something in your Business Central setup is likely to hurt you in the first quarter, and a Rescue Audit is a sensible next step. Two or fewer no answers means routine housekeeping will do.

It is New Year's Eve, which is either the best or the worst time to examine your ERP. The champagne will wait; the Q1 problems will not. This test takes twenty minutes. Answer honestly and count. If "no" appears more than twice, you have found your first project of the year.

What is the ten-question self-audit?

Answer yes or no to each:

  1. Do two of your own staff hold administrator access?
  2. Have all leavers from the past year been removed from Business Central?
  3. Can you post an invoice, a purchase and a payment without setup errors?
  4. Are required dimensions filled in on postings?
  5. Was the last bank reconciliation completed on time?
  6. Does your receivables ledger match the balance sheet account?
  7. Are the extensions you pay for all in use and up to date?
  8. Do key reports match the ledger without manual fixes in Excel?
  9. Do you have a sandbox, and have you tested at least one update in it?
  10. Does someone own each of master data, security and the chart of accounts?

How do you read the result?

Count the no answers. Zero to two: do routine housekeeping. Three or more: book a review, because the problems tend to be linked.

A single "no" in questions 3, 4 or 6 deserves attention on its own. Those touch the ledger directly.

Why do three or more no answers matter?

Because problems cluster. A weak foundation shows up as posting errors, then as report fixes, then as distrust of the numbers. The questions above are symptoms that usually share a few causes.

What should you do with the result?

Write your no answers down, with an owner and a date for each. Fix the quick ones in January. For the rest, get an outside view, so you do not spend a quarter treating symptoms.

Taylor’s Take

Do not wait for the problem that forces your hand. A short self-audit at the turn of the year catches most issues while they are small. (And if question 10 gave you a pause, congratulations: you have found a very common finding.)

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this a replacement for a professional audit?

No. It is a first screen. It tells you where to look and whether to get help.

Who should answer the questions?

A finance lead and the system administrator together.

How often should I repeat it?

Twice a year, and before major updates.

What does a Rescue Audit add?

An independent view of the foundation, with a prioritized fix plan.
