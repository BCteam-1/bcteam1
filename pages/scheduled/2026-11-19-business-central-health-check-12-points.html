A 12-Point Business Central Health Check You Can Run This Week

Quick answer

A Business Central health check reviews twelve areas: access, setup, data, reporting and housekeeping. You can run a first pass yourself in a week by answering pass or fail for each. Most failures are one-day fixes, such as tidying user access. A few, like a redesigned chart of accounts, are projects. Count the fails to decide whether to get outside help.

A finance manager reads "ERP health check" and assumes it needs a consultant and a month. The first pass does not. Twelve questions, an afternoon with the right screens open, and a pass or fail for each. The list below is ours, not a Microsoft standard, and the thresholds are guidelines. Use it to find where to look, then bring in help for the fails that matter.

What does a Business Central health check look at?

It looks at whether your system is secure, set up cleanly, holding clean data, reporting reliably and tidy enough to update. Each area below gets a pass or fail.

What are the twelve checks?

Mark each pass or fail.

#CheckPass looks like
1User accessEvery user has a role that fits the job, and leavers are removed
2Admin accessAt least two of your own staff are administrators
3Chart of accountsNo unused accounts piling up; clear numbering; owner named
4Posting groupsNo recurring setup errors when posting
5DimensionsRequired dimensions filled in on postings
6Bank reconciliationReconciled up to the last month-end
7Open entriesOld unapplied payments and stale orders reviewed this quarter
8Master dataNo obvious duplicate customers, vendors or items
9ReportsKey reports match the ledger and nobody rebuilds them in Excel
10ExtensionsEach has an owner, a reason and a current version
11Job queueNo failed scheduled tasks
12Updates and backupsSandbox available; updates tested before they reach production

Which fails are a one-day fix?

Items 1, 2, 7, 10 and 11 are usually quick. You are removing users, adding an administrator, closing old entries, retiring extensions and restarting failed tasks. A competent administrator can finish them in a day or two.

Which fails are a project?

Items 3, 4, 5 and 9 can take weeks. Redesigning posting groups or dimensions touches history and reports. Plan these with your accountant and a partner, and do not change them mid-month.

Is a paid health check worth it?

It is worth it when you cannot get through the list alone, or when you fail four or more points. An outside review sees what a long-time user has stopped noticing. If you pass nearly everything, a paid review adds little.

What should you do with the results?

Write the fails down, sort them by effort and risk, and fix the quick ones first. Re-run the check in six months. Treat it as a routine, not a one-off.

Taylor’s Take

A health check is only useful if you act on it. Start with the foundation items: chart of accounts, posting groups and dimensions. Fixing a report on top of a weak foundation fixes it once and breaks it again later.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I run a health check?

Once or twice a year, and before a major update or a change in the business.

Can I run it without a consultant?

Yes, for a first pass. Bring in help for the fails you cannot explain.

Does a health check change my data?

No. It is a review. Any fixes are separate steps you approve.

What should I prepare?

A list of users and roles, your installed extensions, last month's bank reconciliation status and the reports you rely on.
