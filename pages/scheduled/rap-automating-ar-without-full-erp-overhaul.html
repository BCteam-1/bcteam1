Automating AR Without a Full ERP Overhaul: How RAP Fits Into Business Central

"AR automation" doesn't mean replacing your ERP. Here's how RAP runs natively alongside Business Central, with no migration and no parallel system.

A CFO hears "AR automation" and assumes it means ripping out the current AR process and replacing it with something new and complicated — another system to learn, another integration project, another few months of disruption before any benefit shows up. That assumption is usually wrong, and it's the single biggest reason companies that would clearly benefit from automating collections never actually look into it seriously.

Automating AR Doesn't Mean Replacing Your ERP

RAP — the Receivables Automation Platform — runs natively alongside Business Central, reading directly from your existing Customer Ledger Entries rather than requiring a separate system with its own customer and invoice data to maintain and keep in sync. There's no data migration, no parallel system to reconcile against your ERP, and no multi-month implementation timeline — it deploys against the environment you're already running.

What Actually Gets Automated

Three things, specifically. Continuous monitoring of aging and payment status, replacing a manual report someone has to remember to pull. Automatic statement generation with the original invoice PDF attached every time, directly eliminating the most common reason customers delay payment — a missing or hard-to-find invoice copy. And rules-based escalation, so a gentle reminder at 15 days and a firmer notice at 45 days happen consistently, on schedule, without depending on someone in AR remembering to run the process manually that week.

Why "Resend the Invoice" Is the Hidden Cost Nobody Calculates

Every time a customer replies asking for the original invoice to be resent, that payment's timeline resets — and in a manual process, this happens constantly, because invoices get lost, filed in the wrong folder, or simply never made it to the right person the first time. This delay compounds across every account it happens to, and it's rarely tracked as its own line item, which is exactly why most companies underestimate how much of their DSO problem traces back to this single, specific failure point.

What Your AR Team's Time Actually Shifts Toward

The realistic outcome of automating the routine 80% of collections work isn't eliminating the AR role — it's shifting that person's time toward the accounts that genuinely need judgment: a customer disputing a charge, a payment plan negotiation, a relationship that needs a phone call instead of an automated reminder. Automation handles the repetitive, mechanical part; a person handles the part that actually requires a person.

A Simple Way to Estimate Your Own Cost of Manual Collections

Multiply the hours per week your team spends on aging reports, reminder drafting, and invoice re-sending by a fully loaded hourly rate — salary, benefits, overhead, not just base pay. Then estimate your current DSO against a realistic target if follow-up never lapsed even once, and calculate what that improvement is worth in freed-up working capital using (annual revenue divided by 365) per day of DSO reduction. Most companies find both numbers larger than they expected once actually calculated, rather than estimated informally.

Where to Start

If your AR process depends on someone remembering to run a report and manually drafting reminders, the realistic first step isn't a system evaluation — it's calculating what the current manual process is actually costing in labor hours and DSO, using your own real numbers. That calculation alone usually makes the case for automating the routine part of collections clearer than any product comparison would.
