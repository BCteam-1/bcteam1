Fix It or Start Over? Business Central Optimization vs Reimplementation

Quick answer

Optimize Business Central when the foundation is sound and the problems sit on top of it: reports, workflows, training and extensions. Reimplement when the foundation itself is wrong: a chart of accounts, dimensions and posting setup that cannot support the reporting you need. Five yes or no questions below point you to one or the other.

Today is US Thanksgiving, so this one is short and meant for later. The question comes up in every struggling project: do we fix this or burn it down? Burning it down feels decisive. It is also the most expensive way to find out you only had a reporting problem. Answer five questions first.

How do you decide between optimizing and reimplementing?

Look at the foundation. If the core design is right and the pain is in how people use it, optimize. If the design cannot give you the numbers you need, rebuilding the core may be cheaper than patching it.

What are the five questions?

Answer yes or no:

  1. Does the chart of accounts support the reports management needs?
  2. Do your dimensions let you slice results the way the business thinks?
  3. Do posting groups work without regular fixes?
  4. Is the data in the system mostly right, and would you trust it for decisions?
  5. Do your users understand the system well enough that training could close the gaps?

Four or five yes answers: optimize. Two or fewer: a deeper review is needed, and a partial or full rebuild may be justified. Three: it depends on which ones are no.

What drives the cost of optimizing?

Optimization costs depend on how many separate problems there are and how much testing each needs. Typical work covers setup cleanup, report fixes, role-based training, workflow changes and extension review.

It is usually done in stages, so you can stop when the value drops.

What drives the cost of reimplementing?

Reimplementation costs come from redesign, data migration, testing and retraining. You pay for work you may have partly paid for already. It is also slower, since users go through change again.

Reimplementation is right when the foundation is the problem. It is a poor answer to a training problem.

What if you are not sure?

Ask for an independent assessment, from a partner who is not selling the rebuild. The result should tell you which parts are sound, which are not and what each path would involve.

Taylor’s Take

Systems are rarely beyond saving. Most "failed" projects have a sound core wrapped in poor decisions on top. Prove the core is wrong before you spend money starting over. (Yes, we have also heard "let's just start fresh" more often than we have heard "let's just read the setup first".)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you partly reimplement?

Yes. Some companies rebuild only the chart of accounts and dimensions, then migrate history. That can cost less than a full restart.

Will reimplementing fix poor user adoption?

Not by itself. Adoption depends on training, roles and process. A new system with the same habits reproduces the old problems.

How long does an assessment take?

It depends on the size of the system. Ask the assessor for a scope and timeline before you begin.

Who should decide?

The business owner, with input from finance and the partner. Do not let the party selling the work decide alone.
