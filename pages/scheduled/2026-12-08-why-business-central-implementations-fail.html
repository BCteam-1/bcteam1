Why Business Central Implementations Fail: Five Decisions Made in the First 30 Days

Quick answer

Business Central implementations most often fail because of five design decisions made in the first 30 days: the chart of accounts, the dimensions, the posting groups, the item setup and who owns the data. Software bugs are rarely the cause. These foundation choices decide what every later report can say, and they are the hardest to change after go-live.

Teams blame failed ERP projects on the software, the partner or the schedule. Look closer and most trace back to a design meeting in week two where nobody pushed back. The foundation determines everything: the chart of accounts, dimensions and posting setup decide what every later report can show. Here are the five decisions to get right, and what to ask in each.

Which five decisions matter most?

They are the chart of accounts, dimensions, posting groups, item setup and data ownership. Each one is hard to change once transactions exist.

Decision 1: What does the chart of accounts need to report?

Design the chart of accounts from the reports you need, not from the old chart. Ask what management, lenders and tax filings require, and build backward.

A copied chart of accounts brings its old problems along.

Decision 2: Which dimensions will you track?

Dimensions are the tags that let you report by department, project, location or other cuts. Decide which ones you need, which are required on every posting and who maintains the values.

Too few and you cannot answer questions. Too many and users enter them wrong. See our dimensions post for the decision process.

Decision 3: How will posting groups map to accounts?

Posting groups decide which account each transaction hits. Design them with your accountant and test each combination. Messy groups produce posting errors and wrong reports for years.

Decision 4: How will items be set up?

For companies with inventory, item types, units of measure, costing method and categories matter. Changing costing after transactions exist is painful. Agree the rules before you load items.

Decision 5: Who owns the data?

Every master data set needs an owner: customers, vendors, items and the chart of accounts. Without owners, duplicates and bad records grow after go-live.

Name the owners in the project plan, with authority to approve changes.

How do you catch a bad decision early?

Run a test with real scenarios before go-live. Post a sale, a purchase, a payment and a month-end close, and read the reports. If a report cannot answer a question it must answer, the foundation needs work.

Taylor’s Take

The foundation determines everything. Spend a week more on design and save months of repair. Any partner who rushes through these five decisions to reach the demo is borrowing against your future.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I change these decisions after go-live?

Some, with effort and risk. Changing a chart of accounts or dimensions after postings exist requires careful planning with your accountant.

Who should make these decisions?

The business owner and finance lead, advised by the partner. They are business decisions, not technical ones.

Is poor training a cause of failure?

Yes, often alongside the design problems. Both need attention.

What is the earliest warning sign?

Design decisions made without finance in the room.
