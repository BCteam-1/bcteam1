5 Signs Your Business Central Environment Needs an Optimization Review (Even If Nothing's "Broken")

No error messages, no failed postings — just a slow accumulation of friction everyone's adapted to instead of fixing. Here are the 5 signs worth checking.

Nothing about the Business Central environment is technically broken. No error messages, no failed postings, no angry support tickets escalating to leadership. And yet month-end takes longer than it used to, a few specific reports get avoided rather than trusted, and someone on the finance team has quietly been keeping a personal spreadsheet "just to double check." None of this shows up as a problem in the way a system outage would — it shows up as a slow accumulation of friction that everyone's adapted to instead of fixing.

This is exactly the pattern optimization engagements are built to catch — not emergencies, but the quieter erosion that happens when an environment goes untouched for years while the business around it keeps changing.

Sign 1: Month-End Takes Longer Than It Used To, and Nobody's Sure Why

A close that's crept from 5 days to 12 over a few years, with no single dramatic cause anyone can point to, is a classic sign of accumulated small inefficiencies — a few extra reconciliation steps here, a manual workaround there — compounding gradually rather than announcing themselves as one clear problem.

Sign 2: Specific Reports or Pages Get Quietly Avoided

If certain screens or reports have developed a reputation internally for being slow, confusing, or unreliable — and people have simply adapted by avoiding them or building workarounds — that's a sign of unresolved friction that's become normalized rather than fixed.

Sign 3: Parallel Spreadsheets Have Become Permanent

A spreadsheet that exists "just to double check" a system report, maintained indefinitely rather than as a temporary bridge during a transition, signals that trust in the system's own numbers has quietly eroded — usually for a specific, identifiable reason worth diagnosing rather than permanently working around.

Sign 4: Setup Decisions From Implementation Have Never Been Revisited

Dimensions, Chart of Accounts structure, permission sets — covered in more depth in related articles on this site — configured once during implementation and never reviewed since, even as the business has genuinely changed in size, complexity, or structure since then. A setup that made sense for a 20-person company doesn't necessarily still make sense at 75 people.

Sign 5: New Hires Take Longer Than Expected to Get Comfortable in the System

If training a new finance or operations hire consistently takes longer than seems reasonable, and experienced staff frequently find themselves explaining workarounds rather than standard processes, that's a sign the system's actual day-to-day usage has drifted from how it was originally designed to be used.

Why "Nothing's Broken" Isn't the Same as "Nothing Needs Attention"

Optimization engagements exist precisely because most performance and efficiency problems don't announce themselves as emergencies — they accumulate quietly, get normalized by the people living with them daily, and only become visible when someone from outside the day-to-day looks at the environment with fresh eyes and actual diagnostic data, rather than assuming a lack of visible errors means everything is fine.

Where to Start

Recognizing two or more of these five signs is a reasonable trigger for a genuine diagnostic — not a sales conversation, an actual evidence-based assessment of what's contributing to the friction your team has adapted to rather than fixed.
