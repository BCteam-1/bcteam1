How to Calculate the Cash You Can Free by Cutting DSO

Quick answer

To estimate the cash freed by cutting DSO, divide your average daily credit sales by one day, then multiply by the number of days you cut. If you sell $10,000 of credit sales per day and cut DSO by 5 days, you free about $50,000 once. This is a one-time release of working capital, not a yearly saving.

A CFO hears "we can cut DSO by a few days" and asks the right question: what is that worth in dollars? The answer is simple arithmetic, and it is worth doing before any tool or hiring decision. The example below uses made-up numbers. Replace them with your own.

How do you turn DSO days into dollars?

Multiply average daily credit sales by the number of days of DSO you remove. Average daily credit sales is annual credit sales divided by 365, or by the number of days in your period.

Each day of DSO you remove is roughly one day of sales that becomes cash earlier.

What does a worked example look like?

This example is hypothetical. Use it to check your method.

ItemHypothetical value
Annual credit sales$3,650,000
Average daily credit sales$10,000
Current DSO52 days
Target DSO47 days
Days removed5
Cash freed (one time)$50,000

The $50,000 is not profit. It is cash that was tied up in receivables and is now available.

What is the cash worth?

Put a value on it by asking what you would do with the money. If you carry a line of credit, the saving is the interest you avoid on that amount. If you invest in inventory or payroll timing, the value is the flexibility.

Do not claim the freed cash every year. You release it once. After that, the gain is that you keep the lower balance.

What should you check before you trust the number?

Check three things: whether your DSO calculation uses credit sales only, whether seasonal swings distort it, and whether the target is realistic given your payment terms. Customers on 45-day terms will not average 30 days.

Also check which customers drive the balance. One large account can hold most of the opportunity.

How do you decide whether to invest in improving it?

Compare the cash value with the cost of the effort: staff time, a tool, or both. For a tool, ask for a written price and run the same sum with your own balance. The result should tell you the payback in months, not a promise.

Taylor’s Take

Calculate the prize before you buy the shovel. If the freed cash is small, a better routine may be enough. If it is large, you can justify real investment in the process. Either way, use your own numbers, not a vendor's example.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is freed cash the same as saved cost?

No. It is working capital released once. The ongoing benefit is the lower balance you carry.

Should I use total sales or credit sales?

Credit sales. Cash sales do not create receivables.

How fast can DSO improve?

It depends on your starting process and customers. Set a target by customer group, not a single number.

Does cutting DSO hurt customer relationships?

It can if follow-up is clumsy. A clear policy and polite, consistent reminders usually protect relationships.
