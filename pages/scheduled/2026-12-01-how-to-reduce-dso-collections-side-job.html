How to Reduce DSO When Collections Is One Person's Side Job

Quick answer

To reduce days sales outstanding (DSO), track two numbers next to it: the percentage of receivables that is past due, and the share of promises to pay that were kept. DSO is slow to move and hides the cause. These two show where follow-up is failing, and both can be built from Business Central customer ledger entries.

DSO is the number everyone watches and nobody can move directly. It is an average, so it lags. By the time it rises, the problem is weeks old. When collections is a side job, squeezed between month-end and everything else, the fix starts with better numbers and a fixed weekly routine. Not heroics.

What is DSO and why does it hide problems?

Days sales outstanding estimates how many days it takes to collect payment after a sale. A common formula is receivables divided by credit sales, multiplied by the number of days in the period. Use the version your finance team already agrees on.

It hides problems because one slow-paying large customer or one month of heavy sales moves it. You see the result, not the cause.

Which two numbers should you track beside it?

Track the percent of receivables past due and the promise-kept rate. The first shows how much money is late. The second shows whether follow-up works.

  • Percent past due: overdue receivables divided by total open receivables.
  • Promise-kept rate: promises to pay that were honored on the promised date, divided by promises made. You need to record promises for this one.

How do you pull them from Business Central?

Start with open customer ledger entries and the due date. Filter for entries with a due date before today, total them, and divide by total open entries. The Aged Accounts Receivable report gives a similar view by age bucket.

Promises to pay are not tracked by default in a standard report. Teams often use a note, a comment or a spreadsheet. If yours lives in a spreadsheet, that is a gap worth closing.

What weekly routine moves the numbers?

Run the same short routine each week:

  1. Pull the past-due list sorted by amount and age.
  2. Contact the top accounts first, and record each promise with a date.
  3. Check last week's promises and follow up on any that slipped.
  4. Review disputes and assign each an owner.
  5. Record the two numbers so you can see the trend.

Thirty focused minutes beats a monthly marathon.

When does the side-job model stop working?

It stops working when volume grows, or when the person doing it is pulled to close. Signs are promises nobody checks and reminders sent late. At that point, tooling or a dedicated owner is cheaper than the late cash.

Taylor’s Take

A DSO target without a weekly routine is a wish. Pick the two supporting numbers, assign an owner and review them every week. Software helps with the routine. It cannot replace having one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good DSO?

It varies by industry and payment terms. Compare against your own terms and your own trend first.

Can Business Central calculate DSO?

You can build it from customer ledger entries and sales figures, or through a report your partner configures.

How do I record promises to pay?

Use a consistent place: a comment on the customer or entry, a log, or a tool that tracks them. Include date and amount.

Is it worth chasing small balances?

Set a threshold in your policy. Automate small ones and give people time for the large ones.
