How to Merge Duplicate Customers and Vendors in Business Central

Quick answer

Open the customer or vendor card you want to keep, choose Merge With, and select the duplicate. Review the fields that differ, choose which values to keep, resolve any conflicts and merge. Merging cannot be undone, and only users with the MERGE DUPLICATES permission set can do it. Back up or test in a sandbox first.

Acme Ltd, ACME Limited and Acme (old). Three cards, one customer, and a collector who never knows which one to open. Duplicates split history, confuse reports and cause duplicate payments. Business Central has a built-in merge. It is powerful and it is permanent, so the preparation matters more than the clicks.

Can you merge customer and vendor records?

Yes. Business Central has a Merge With action on customer, vendor and contact cards. It combines a duplicate into the record you keep and updates references to the duplicate's number.

Microsoft's documentation says merging cannot be undone, and requires the MERGE DUPLICATES permission set.

What are the steps?

Follow Microsoft's steps:

  1. Open the record you want to keep, for example the customer card.
  2. Choose Merge With and select the duplicate.
  3. On the Merge Duplicate page, review the Fields section. It shows only fields whose values differ. A few differences, such as typos, suggest a true duplicate. Many differences suggest it may not be one.
  4. Check the Related Tables section, which shows record counts for each side. It is for information only.
  5. For each field where you want the duplicate's value, choose Override.
  6. Choose Merge and confirm.

What are conflicts and how do you handle them?

A conflict exists when related records share the same key but belong to different numbers. If conflicts appear, resolve them before the merge completes. For each conflict you can remove the duplicate's record or rename it so both can be kept.

Read each conflict carefully. Removing a record deletes it.

What should you check before merging?

Check three things. First, are they truly the same company? Second, what is open on each, such as invoices, orders or payments? Third, do any integrations or external systems refer to the old number?

Microsoft's article does not explicitly address ledger entries. Confirm how your history looks after a test merge in a sandbox, and ask your partner if unsure.

How do you prevent new duplicates?

Search before you create. Agree naming rules, such as legal name first, no abbreviations. Limit who can create customers and vendors. Review new records weekly.

Taylor’s Take

Master data is the cheapest thing to keep clean and the most expensive to fix late. Make "search before you create" a rule, and review new cards each week. Your future self, and your auditors, will thank you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I undo a merge?

No. Microsoft states that merging cannot be undone.

Does merging combine invoices?

Related records are updated to refer to the record you keep. Test in a sandbox to see how your history appears.

Who can merge?

Users with the MERGE DUPLICATES permission set.

Does this work for contacts?

Yes, the same action exists for contacts, and there is a separate duplicate search for them.
