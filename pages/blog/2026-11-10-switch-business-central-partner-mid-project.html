Can You Switch Business Central Partners Mid-Project?

Quick answer

Yes, you can switch Business Central partners mid-project, because your Microsoft tenant, environments and data belong to you. The risk is what the old partner holds that you cannot see: admin access, extension source code and the reasons behind each setup choice. Collect those in writing before you give notice.

A go-live date has slipped twice. Your partner has sent a third change order. Someone on your finance team finally asks what nobody wanted to say: can you switch Business Central partners and keep going? You can. But the partner may hold the only admin login, the only copy of your custom code and the only notes on why the chart of accounts looks the way it does. Get those first. The sections below show what you own, what to request, and the order to do it in.

Who owns what in a Business Central project?

You own your Microsoft 365 tenant, your Business Central environments and the data inside them. A partner may control other things through delegated access, a contract or a subscription they resell.

How do you switch Business Central partner without losing your work?

Request the handover package in writing first, get an independent assessment second, and give notice third. Reversing that order leaves you negotiating for access after the relationship has soured.

  1. Read the termination and intellectual property clauses in your contract. Have your lawyer confirm what you can ask for. This is not legal advice.
  2. Ask for the handover package, in writing, before you give notice. Use the list in the next section.
  3. Get an independent assessment from a second partner. You are checking what is configured and why, not assigning blame.
  4. Confirm two people on your team hold administrator access, then review which partner accounts still have it.
  5. Freeze configuration changes and go-live dates until the assessment is done. Low-risk work such as data cleanup can continue.
  6. Schedule knowledge-transfer sessions between the old and new teams, with your process owners in the room.
  7. Give notice. Remove the old partner’s access once the transfer is signed off, not before.
  8. Rebuild the project plan with the new partner, using the assessment findings.

What should a Business Central handover package contain?

Ask for eight things. Each is something a new partner needs on day one and cannot reconstruct from memory.

  • Admin and user list: every account with admin rights, and every delegated partner relationship.
  • Environment list: production and sandbox environments, their versions and update settings. The Business Central admin center lists them.
  • Extension inventory: the Extension Management page shows installed extensions, publishers and versions. Add who owns each one’s source code.
  • Source code: repository access for every per-tenant extension.
  • Configuration record: chart of accounts, dimensions, posting groups, number series and approval workflows, plus the reason for each decision.
  • Data migration files: mapping sheets, scripts, import logs and unresolved data issues.
  • Open items: the issue list and every signed or pending change order.
  • License and subscription details: who bills for what, and when each term renews.

When is switching partners the wrong move?

Switching is the wrong move when the problem sits on your side of the table. A new Business Central consultant inherits missing decisions and unavailable staff along with the Business Central implementation.

Signs the partner is the problem:

  • Milestones are missed with no revised plan.
  • Staff on your project change every few weeks.
  • Nobody can explain why the system is configured the way it is.
  • The same defect returns after being closed.

Signs the problem is internal:

  • Requirements change monthly and no one owns the decision.
  • Process owners skip workshops.
  • Data owners have not signed off on cleanup.
  • Scope keeps growing after sign-off.

If both lists apply, fix your side first. Otherwise the next partner hits the same wall.

Taylor’s Take

Do not start with the fixes. Start with the foundation. Taylor’s core belief is that “the foundation determines everything”: the chart of accounts, dimensions and posting groups decide what every later report says. So the first thing a new partner should give you is a written description of that foundation, not a list of things they plan to change. If a partner offers to start fixing before they can describe it, ask why.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I switch Business Central partners while still under contract?

Usually yes, but the termination clause sets the notice period and any fees. Ask your lawyer to read it before you send notice.

Will I lose my data if I switch partners?

No. Your data lives in your Business Central environment, in your tenant. The risk is losing access if the partner holds the only admin account, so confirm your own admin access first.

How long does a partner handover take?

It depends on how many extensions you have and how well the setup is documented. Ask the new partner to estimate after they review the extension list and the configuration record.

Should the project stop during the switch?

Freeze configuration changes and go-live dates. Low-risk work such as data cleanup and user list review can continue.
