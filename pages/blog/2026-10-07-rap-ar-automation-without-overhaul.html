Automating AR Without a Full ERP Overhaul: How RAP Fits Into Business Central

You don't need a new ERP project to fix a broken collections process. Here's how RAP runs alongside what you already have.

A CFO frustrated with slow collections and rising DSO often assumes the fix requires a bigger project — a new module, a system upgrade, a lengthy implementation with its own budget cycle and timeline. That assumption is usually wrong, and it's expensive in a specific way: it delays fixing a problem that's actively costing cash flow every single week it goes unaddressed, while everyone waits for a "real" project to get budgeted and scheduled.

The AR automation problem — inconsistent follow-up, invoices that don't attach properly, statements that go out late or not at all — doesn't require touching your core ERP configuration. It requires a rules engine sitting on top of data your Business Central environment already has.

Why This Doesn't Need to Be an ERP Project

Business Central already tracks every open invoice, every payment, every aging bucket accurately inside the Customer Ledger Entries table. The data required to automate collections isn't missing — it's just not being acted on automatically. This is precisely why fixing it doesn't require reconfiguring your core financial setup, migrating data, or touching anything that would normally trigger a change-management review.

RAP connects to that existing data and runs the mechanical parts of collections — monitoring aging, sending statements, attaching the correct invoice — without altering how Business Central itself is configured underneath.

What Actually Gets Deployed

Deployment means connecting RAP to your existing environment and configuring the rules that matter to your business — reminder cadence, escalation thresholds, which accounts need special handling. This is meaningfully different from an implementation project: there's no data migration, no user retraining on core BC functionality, and no disruption to how your team already works inside Business Central day to day.

Why Speed Matters Here Specifically

Unlike a lot of ERP-adjacent decisions, delaying an AR automation fix has a direct, compounding cost: every week collections stays manual and inconsistent is another week of invoices drifting further into aging, cash sitting uncollected longer than it needs to, and staff time spent on repetitive follow-up instead of genuine exceptions. A fast deployment path isn't just a convenience — it's directly tied to how quickly the actual financial benefit starts showing up.

What This Looks Like in the First 30 Days

In practice, most of the value shows up quickly once RAP is connected: statements start going out consistently and automatically include the correct invoice, the AR team stops manually drafting routine follow-up emails, and the specific friction point — customers replying asking for a document that should have been attached — starts disappearing almost immediately, because the automated statement never omits it in the first place.

The Actual Decision in Front of You

The real choice isn't "automate AR now versus as part of a bigger future project." It's "keep absorbing the cost of manual, inconsistent collections for however many more months until a bigger project gets budgeted, versus fixing the specific, isolated problem now, on its own timeline." Framed that way, waiting for a larger initiative rarely makes financial sense when the fix itself doesn't require one.
